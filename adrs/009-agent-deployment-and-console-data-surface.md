## Context

A grilling on the CRM's `docs/api.md` — 592 lines of incident-born rules — surfaced six questions for our architecture. One answer reverses a standing decision: ADR-003 chose `withEve()` mounted in `apps/app`; the Q1 discussion (the no-intelligence line loses its physical enforcement when agent and console share a process) led to adopting the CRM's topology instead.

The first formulation of this ADR put the CMO and all named specialist roots in one standalone `apps/agent` deployment. Further implementation review rejected that multi-agent mount: the intended topology is one real eve application and Vercel deployment per root, with shared definitions preventing the two materializations of each specialist from drifting.

The normative runtime surface for this ADR is eve 0.31.3. In particular, public sessions are addressed by their immutable `session_id`, while authored custom channels own their channel-local addresses and expose `from(address).send(...)` inside route handlers.

## Decision

### 1. Seven standalone root apps; only the CMO is user-addressable (amends ADR-003)

- The monorepo has exactly **seven eve root applications and deployments**: `agent-cmo` plus one for each of the six specialist roots (`product-marketer`, `content`, `distribution`, `seo-discovery`, `lifecycle`, and `growth`). They are not named agents mounted under one eve application.
- `agent-cmo` authors the normal `eveChannel` with bridge authentication and is the only user-addressable root. `apps/app` never mounts eve; its authenticated same-origin `/eve/v1/*` proxy targets **only `agent-cmo`**.
- The six specialist roots are machine-only. Because eve 0.31.3 enables its default HTTP channel even when `agent/channels/eve.ts` is absent, each specialist explicitly authors that file as `eveChannel({ auth: [localDev()] })`. Consequently `/eve/v1/session*` and `/eve/v1/info` are available to local development tooling but return `401` in production; `/eve/v1/health` remains public for health checks. Omitting the file is not equivalent: the framework default does not express this production fail-closed policy.
- **The bridge** follows the CRM pattern: browser → `/eve/v1/*` (same origin, session cookie) → a proxy route in `apps/app` checks the Better Auth session, strips the cookie, mints a short-lived HS256 token naming the human principal with `CMO_BRIDGE_SECRET` → the CMO root's `/eve/v1/*`. Only `apps/app` and `agent-cmo` receive this credential. The CMO remaps the token to a real user principal (their `repFromCrm` pattern): eve's `jwtHmac()` alone resolves to `principalType: "service"`, while the CMO needs the human identity for tenant ACLs, read capabilities, and Actor attribution. `apps/app` rereads the exact Better Auth Member and its current role on every proxy or application request; a role from an earlier bridge JWT, session, browser state, or conversation is never reused as authorization for the next request. External-commitment approval remains an `apps/app` transaction authenticated directly by Better Auth (ADR-019).
- **The CMO proxy is conversation-aware on the control plane and transparent on the data plane.** A CMO conversation is created before its first send, and that product mutation accepts only current Member role `owner | admin | member`. The browser's `useEveAgent` store owns the live `ClientSession`, cursor, response-stream consumption, and reconnect loop. Its `POST /eve/v1/session` carries the application `conversation_id` only as a proxy-local untrusted selector; before forwarding, the proxy resolves that row and brand, verifies the Better Auth user is its `owner_user_id`, and requires the exact current `Member(brand.organization_id, user_id)` with role `owner | admin | member`. A prior authorized row creation or token does not satisfy this first-turn check. Only the create handler reads the selector, while the proxy strips it from every forwarded create, follow-up, stream, or reconnect request. It mints server-derived brand/conversation auth claims, forwards the request, validates the returned `x-eve-session-id`, and fill-or-match binds that id before exposing the response. A conflicting id or failed binding is not exposed. The proxy preserves upstream status, non-hop-by-hop headers, body, and stream semantics; it never consumes the later NDJSON stream or owns reconnect policy.
- Every current and future route below `/eve/v1/session/:sessionId` is fail-closed and classified before forwarding. All classes require the exact `cmo_conversations.owner_user_id`, exact current Member, brand, and stored `session_id`; v1 has no cross-member read ACL or organization-role override. Transcript, snapshot, and stream are read operations and remain available when that exact owner is now a `viewer`, while every other user is denied even when they are an owner/admin Member of the same organization. Follow-up `message` or `inputResponses`, compact, clear, and reset are turn/session mutations and require the exact owner with current role `owner | admin | member`. The thin cursor checkpoint and every product mutation enforce that same write-role set at their own `apps/app` boundary. Cancel is the sole viewer exception: the exact current owner who still has a `viewer` Member may `POST` an observed non-empty `turnId` to `/cancel`; Eve scopes it to that turn, so a stale or different id is a no-op and cannot stop an unseen later turn. The cancel response grants no follow-up, HITL, cursor, or product authority. Unknown future subroutes default to deny until assigned to a class. A missing row, unknown id, mismatched conversation, wrong organization, non-owner, or unauthorized caller fails without reaching Eve; knowledge of a conversation, session, or turn id is never authority. There is no separate `brand_memberships` table or lookup; authorization uses the organization Member plus the brand's `organization_id`.
- The CMO's top-level `session.started` hook and the proxy response path perform the same fill-or-match conversation binding; child hooks are rejected. Browser `onSessionChange`/`onFinish` callbacks checkpoint only the exact bound id and, for current `owner | admin | member`, update `stream_index` monotonically through a thin application mutation. An exact owner downgraded to `viewer` may consume its transcript, snapshot, or live stream, but neither its callbacks nor a forged request may mutate that checkpoint. Reload does not trust the checkpoint. Before mounting the hook, an authorized snapshot read starts at index `0`; if a turn is still active, a temporary low-level client drains from the snapshot cursor and explicitly stops at the first `isCurrentTurnBoundaryEvent`. Hook ingestion is best effort and persistence failures alert; snapshot/recovery remains read-only with respect to `session_events` and never reconciles a miss. Only the combined events and final cursor hydrate the read surface; unavailable Eve history falls back to the full cursor-paginated persisted prefix emitted by that exact bound root, folded in stored order through Eve 0.31.3's `defaultMessageReducer()`, and that fallback may contain gaps. `apps/app` and `agent-cmo` pin that exact Eve version and upgrade together behind a stored-event reducer fixture.
- An owner-viewer request never reaches a special read-only CMO turn. V1 does not invoke the model and then try to remove write tools through a prompt, role-conditioned tool list, or filtered subagent surface; the proxy rejects every viewer send/HITL request before `agent-cmo`, while owner-authorized read routes remain ordinary data-plane reads.
- Record context travels **in the token claims, never prefixed into the user's message**.
- Every root authors a machine-only custom `dispatch` channel whose route table contains `POST /internal/dispatch`. The route authenticates a raw `Authorization: Bearer $DISPATCH_SECRET` and fails closed when the configured secret or bearer is absent or invalid. `DISPATCH_SECRET` is present in `apps/app` and all seven roots, but authorizes only this poke route; it is never accepted as a user token and cannot sign the CMO bridge's HS256 human-principal JWTs.
- In a specialist production deployment, `/internal/dispatch` is therefore the only ingress that can create agent work. After an atomic agent-task claim, the custom-channel handler delivers the authoritative brief with `from(taskAddress).send(message, { auth, mode: "task", outputSchema })`; `taskAddress` is derived from the already-trusted task id and is not a public session or continuation token. Only ADR-018's stale unbound-handoff recovery may call `send()` again; a task with an authoritative `session_id` never does. Framework-owned callback routes may remain enabled because they only resume already accepted durable work; they cannot start an unrelated specialist run.
- The architecture does not depend on `disableRoute()` or any other private eve route API. The supported boundary is explicit: only the CMO exposes the default `/eve/v1/session*` surface to the product UI, while specialist `eveChannel({ auth: [localDev()] })` routes fail closed in production and their authenticated custom dispatch route remains available.
- The no-agent-intelligence line is **physical**: the console, CMO, and six specialist roots are separate processes and deployments. Inside each app it is also **mechanical**: Biome restricted imports keep console routes and Server Actions away from agent runtime code. They normally use `packages/brain`; the narrow Context.dev onboarding adapter remains server-only deterministic application code and commits its normalized result through `packages/brain` (ADR-008, ADR-012). Their war story stands as the justification: copied identity matchers drifted silently until one matched every employer on earth; a shared process makes that drift one autocomplete away.
- What stands from ADR-003: the console as primary surface, the four surfaces, and the two carved-in-stone rules. What changes: all eight runtime apps (`app` plus seven roots; `web` is separate) have explicit deployment boundaries from the beginning. An unreachable root is `offline`, not `working`.

### 1a. One shared definition, two specialist materializations

`packages/agents` is the source of truth for every agent identity, core instructions, skills, model defaults, task kinds, and mode-specific surfaces. It exports canonical definitions and reusable eve workspace-extension contributions; each standalone app keeps only thin local wrappers for root-only configuration and mounting. It materializes:

- the CMO root together with all six **consultative declared-subagent** forms inside `agent-cmo`;
- one **durable root wrapper** inside each corresponding specialist app.

The consultative and durable forms intentionally differ in authority and contract (ADR-017), but their shared specialist core is generated from the same entry. Hand-maintained copies are forbidden. Eve extensions may share skills, tools, connections, instruction fragments, and hooks. Agent configuration, custom channels, and sandbox policy remain consumer-local; Branderize defines no Eve filesystem schedules. Product-cadence templates are instead shared application registry metadata compiled into `apps/app` and the responsible roots. The generator therefore calls the shared model-config factory explicitly in every root and declared-subagent `agent.ts`; it is not smuggled through an extension. A self-copy reuses the root config. This is what prevents a root deployment and the CMO's local subagent version of that specialist from drifting without pretending that every eve filesystem slot is extension-mountable.

A schedule-template retirement has one conservative deployment exception to ordinary independent promotion. The commit that changes an active entry into its retired tombstone is promoted to the responsible root first. The release gate records completion of that production routing cutover, verifies the live root source contains the exact retirement commit and then waits a quiescence interval equal to the root Function's deployment-verified Max Duration plus a 30-second release buffer—330 seconds under v1's 300-second contract—before `apps/app` may promote the console manifest that labels the template retired/non-runnable. Thus every invocation admitted by the previous root deployment has exhausted its maximum lifetime before the UI makes the claim. Here non-runnable means that the schedule can materialize no new occurrence; a task accepted before quiescence retains its ordinary visible lifecycle and may still drain under legacy kind support. The old console may temporarily show a setting that the updated root already refuses to execute; the reverse ordering is forbidden. Rollback is the exact inverse and remains enforced for every later deployment, not only the initial release: while the live console contains that tombstone, the gate rejects any responsible-root promotion or rollback whose compiled manifest makes the key executable; operators must roll the console back first and only then the root. A tombstone-compatible root build remains deployable. This uses live deployment source identity, cutover time and the already-verified Function duration plus the build-time root/console manifest fixture, not a product flag or database mutation. A failed initial root promotion leaves the console promotion blocked, while a later emergency rollback preserves the same truthful ordering.

### 1b. One Next.js cron fans out; each root drains only its own work

- A single Vercel Cron invokes a route in `apps/app`. That ingress verifies `Authorization: Bearer $CRON_SECRET`; there are no eve schedules.
- `CRON_SECRET` belongs only to the Vercel cron ingress and the receiving route in `apps/app`. The route never forwards or reuses it; outgoing root pokes use `DISPATCH_SECRET`.
- The route sends seven `POST /internal/dispatch` requests in parallel, one per root. Each request has an independent two-second timeout, and `Promise.allSettled` waits only for the seven acknowledgments. A failed or offline root does not prevent the other six from being poked.
- Each root's custom-channel route validates machine authentication, responds `202 Accepted`, and registers its drain with `waitUntil`. For every claimed agent row, that drain uses the route-scoped `from(taskAddress).send(...)` operation described above. The acknowledgment does not mean the queued work has completed.
- Eve 0.31.3 compiles that custom-channel route into each root app's generated shared Vercel Function. V1 leaves Fluid Compute's duration uncustomized and relies on its 300-second Default Max Duration: it exports no route `maxDuration`, defines no `ROOT_MAX_DURATION`, enables no longer opt-in duration, and applies no post-build patch to `.vc-config.json`. `waitUntil` remains inside the same shared Function and cannot extend its lifetime. Human provider calls use `PROVIDER_HTTP_TIMEOUT = 90s`, while their owner classifies a row only after `STALE_AFTER = 10m`. A project-duration change is a reviewed deployment change and requires an accompanying review/update of `STALE_AFTER`; a deployment or Eve-upgrade check may inspect the resolved Function configuration, but no permanent artifact patcher is part of v1.
- `/internal/dispatch` is a pure wake-up signal. Its contract accepts no `task_id`, `brand_id`, payload, `worker_key`, or target root. The called deployment is the target, and its dispatcher derives `SELF`, `compiledSupportedKinds`, and `compiledScheduleKeysForSelf` from one compiled registry materialization. Before the ordinary drain it first evaluates and advances its own due registered schedules, materializing only origin-free work for a schedule without a queued/running occurrence, then terminalizes only its own stale `direct/human` rows according to ADR-019; it never requeues or re-executes them. New claims filter `worker_key = SELF AND kind IN compiledSupportedKinds` before any lifecycle mutation; possession of `DISPATCH_SECRET` therefore cannot select work or a schedule for a root to execute.
- Schedule materialization follows ADR-014 D7. Before its bounded order, the root filters enabled due rows by `worker_key = SELF`, `schedule_key IN compiledScheduleKeysForSelf`, supported kind, and `next_scheduled_for <= now()`. For each candidate, one transaction exact-matches its registry template, compare-and-swaps the revision and due cursor, advances to the first future wall-clock slot, then locks any `queued | running` occurrence. An active row coalesces. With no active row, current null-structure Policy either creates the exact origin-free `(schedule_id, scheduled_for)` task or records `not_allowed` with zero task; Policy/registry/insert errors roll the whole evaluation back. Only after this bounded pass does the root enter stale classification and the three task lanes.
- Every task carries a registry-derived `worker_key` and `execution_mode: agent | direct`. A root claims only `worker_key = SELF AND kind IN compiledSupportedKinds`, regardless of lane. Agent mode enters that root's eve session; direct mode runs deterministic code inside that same responsible root. In particular, deterministic execution of a human-approved commitment belongs to the root that owns its task, not to a central dispatcher in `apps/app`, the CMO model, or a resumed eve tool.
- A due row whose kind is not compiled into the running root is left completely untouched: no status change, session start, lease, attempt increment, fallback lane, or generic agent brief. A later compatible deployment can claim the same row. Published task-kind payload and output contracts therefore change only backward-compatibly. A breaking change uses a new kind, or an explicit expand-contract rollout in which consumers accept both shapes before producers emit the new one and legacy support is removed only after old rows drain. V1 adds neither a `contract_version` column nor a deployment coordinator.
- A process-local `collapsing()` guard is a best-effort optimization against overlapping drains within one warm instance. Cross-instance correctness comes from atomic Postgres lifecycle transitions. Schedule CAS, exact `(schedule_id, scheduled_for)` replay identity, and partial uniqueness on active `schedule_id` protect materialization; the materializer's task-row lock serializes due-slot evaluation against terminalization. Agent rows claim `queued → running` once; retry-safe direct/automatic rows may use their bounded lease for transactional internal work or side-effect-free idempotent external reads; human external commitments also claim once but have no reclaim after `running`. Duplicate or overlapping cron invocations and pokes are therefore safe; the next cron tick backs up due schedule evaluation and claimable work and lets each healthy root terminalize its own human commitments older than `STALE_AFTER` without another provider call.
- Each root has configured, bounded `STALE_SCAN_LIMIT`, `CANDIDATE_SCAN_LIMIT`, and `DRAIN_BATCH` values; they are runtime configuration, not task priority. Stale classification runs first but stops at its own limit so a backlog cannot consume the ordinary drain. Candidate queries may read and order a bounded set of ids without mutating them: first claimable `direct/human` rows by `execute_before ASC NULLS LAST`, denormalized `approved_at`, then task id; then due `direct/automatic` rows by `created_at, id`; then due agent rows by `created_at, id`. The latter two FIFO queries filter `due_at <= now()` before ordering. Reading ids is not reserving work.
- There is **no direct/human pre-claim batch**. Each available per-invocation worker slot takes one candidate id, completes deterministic preflight while it is still `queued`, performs the one-row `queued -> running` compare-and-swap, and immediately enters that task's registered provider handler. That same slot waits for provider classification and terminal settlement before selecting another id. Expired, incompatible, temporarily blocked, or lost-CAS candidates consume scan/runtime budget but do not consume `DRAIN_BATCH`; the count advances only when an execution is actually claimed and started. The next slot iteration evaluates lane preference again, so there is no frozen mutable batch of `running` commitments waiting for earlier provider calls.
- `DRAIN_BATCH` limits executions actually started by one invocation; a separate small per-invocation concurrency bound limits how many slot loops may be inside claim/handler/settlement at once. Neither is total root concurrency. Fluid Compute may run overlapping invocations or instances, each with its own limit; Postgres gives them disjoint claims, but together they may start more than `DRAIN_BATCH` work globally. This is admission preference at each one-row claim, not a global start/completion order, concurrency semaphore, or serial scheduler.
- This deliberately adapts rather than copies the current CRM's [`RUN_BATCH = 20` plus `createdAt, id` FIFO](https://github.com/trycompai/crm/blob/682ae0f1f7f5c4d2737b72dbf9941e7463693e42/apps/agent/agent/lib/custom-agent-dispatch.ts#L256-L270). Its queue contains homogeneous `AgentRun` rows. Branderize shares a root dispatcher across three lanes, so FIFO remains inside each lower lane while an already-authorized external commitment receives the first claim opportunity. Sustained human-commitment load may starve both lower lanes, and sustained automatic-direct load may starve agent work. V1 accepts and measures both conditions; no quota, weighted scheduler, global semaphore, or persisted priority is added before starvation occurs in practice.
- Per-brand deterministic daily/weekly cadences are disabled registry templates materialized as durable `schedules` rows, separate from the one-shot `tasks` they sometimes create. Only an authenticated human may configure and enable one. Their calendar is wall-clock and IANA-timezone aware; the Vercel cron expression is only a UTC wake-up frequency and encodes no brand cadence. At most one cadence occurrence per schedule may be `queued | running`. A later due evaluation behind it advances the calendar and updates only the schedule's monotonic coalescing summary; it creates no task or backlog. Once the active task terminalizes, no immediate catch-up is synthesized: only the already-computed future cursor may create the next occurrence. The cron route decides no marketing schedule, materializes no occurrence, and dispatches no work itself; it only pokes all seven owners. Agent-authored `scheduleRecheck` remains a separate task-bound future-work mechanism and never edits a deterministic schedule.

### 2. Explicit tenancy, never ambient (Q2)

- `brand_id` is the **first parameter of every `packages/brain` function**. No AsyncLocalStorage tenant context, no ambient scoping: the console resolves org/brand from the URL once at the route boundary and passes it down explicitly. A missing `brand_id` is a type error, not a silent cross-tenant read.
- Slugs are cosmetic prefixes only: one slugify function, a reserved-slug guard (a brand named "settings" gets a suffix), no read takes them, no record carries them.

### 3. The console's data layer (Q3)

- **Server Components read projections** via `packages/brain` reads; **Server Actions** perform the few writes (human `declareIntent`, `refineIntent`, `adoptIntent`, `abandonIntent`, `settleIntent`, `recordDecision`, `approveTask`, `dismissTask`, `configureSchedule`, Plan Rebuild/Ricontrolla/Avvia comunque, settings-via-brain) and the deterministic Context.dev plus schedule-template setup described by ADR-012/ADR-014; zod validates every action and provider response at its boundary. `reconcileBrandSchedules` is internal setup/backfill, not a Server Action or agent tool. Human Intent/Decision/schedule boundaries derive the current Member/User/Actor and never accept author, status, worker, kind, payload, origin, or due cursor. The interactive CMO exposes the same typed refinement operation as a distinct trusted wrapper only for an explicit reply in its current top-level human turn: it derives current Member, CMO Actor, conversation/session/turn/call provenance and operation identity from runtime context, and writes immediately without an Approval Action or second button. That same interactive wrapper alone exposes `request_specialist_work`: the CMO may nominate an exact Intent only when the current human request identifies it without ambiguity, while trusted code reloads current Member/tenant state and constructs the origin snapshot. It also exposes the read-only `present_decision_recommendation` tool described by ADR-020: the tool returns one typed UI part with a trusted stable request id but writes no graph state. For a terminal task-question card, `apps/app` reloads the exact same-brand task and current Member, opens or creates only that user's own writable top-level conversation, and stamps `source_task_id` into trusted current-turn auth attributes; model-visible `clientContext` is presentation only. The wrapper then exposes `resolve_task_questions` only for that turn and source. The CMO receives no durable `recommend_decision` or schedule-configuration write tool, and the Decision card button calls the ordinary human `recordDecision` Server Action with a null source report. Autonomous/task wrappers expose `proposeIntent` drafts and inherited-origin work only; they cannot pass a raw `intent_id`. Plan commands validate current Member/tenant state and enqueue registered CMO work; none runs model intelligence in the console.
- Filtering, sorting, pagination **always in SQL** — never return a whole table and filter in the browser. Sort keys resolve against a per-module column allow-list, never interpolated into field names.
- Thin actions, thick brain. No tRPC, no separate API service.

### 4. Freshness: agent writes are not invalidations (Q4)

- Work the browser did not cause (an agent producing an Object while the human watches) cannot be a cache invalidation — **poll**, gated on active work (`awaiting_approval`, `queued`, or `running` tasks), and stop when settled. **Lists poll too, not just detail views** (their logo-in-the-sheet-but-not-in-the-table bug).
- User-caused mutations invalidate through **one cache module** that owns the fan-out — *"say what changed, not which keys"*; twelve hand-written key lists is how theirs drifted.
- No SSE/websocket in v1: eve already streams sessions for chat; everything else is polling. Reopen in Phase 2 if the digest wants live updates.

### 5. Deletion vs automation — including objects outside branderize (Q5)

- **During a brand's lifetime, grammar history is not rewritten.** Objects supersede or are dismissed, tasks settle, and Actions append. Tenant deletion is the explicit exception: all internal brand-scoped rows have a real foreign-key path to `brands` with `ON DELETE CASCADE` and are hard-deleted together.
- **External manifestations are outside that cascade.** Superseding internal content does not automatically retract, update, or delete anything at a provider. If a correction, replacement, pause, unpublish, cancellation, or close is desired, it is a new registered human-approved commitment task. Previously accepted provider state may remain after brand deletion and become orphaned.
- **External drift is user-owned in v1.** A human editing provider state or switching the active provider account does not mutate our Objects and does not trigger a generic readback/hash or reapproval protocol. The click authorizes the current registered provider operation; the Action and receipt record what Branderize did, not a claim that external state stayed unchanged beforehand.
- **A call already in flight can outlive deletion.** The database prevents any later internal claim because the row no longer exists, but cannot revoke an HTTP request already started. Brand deletion performs no provider cleanup and the dispatcher does not perform an additional “brand still exists” check beside referential integrity.

### 6. Gates surface their accumulation (Q6)

- Every optional capability gets a **visible counter of the work waiting on it** ("3 proposals need Notion connected"). A gate either has no escape hatch or surfaces what the escape accumulates — their Skip button stranded installs with companies `PENDING` forever and nothing saying so.
- The UI's disabled state and the gate's denial read the **same `packages/policy` evaluation** — the button and the 403 can never disagree.
- Credit exhaustion is lane-specific rather than a generic capability denial: its counter covers queued agent-session work. It never disables approve or cancel controls for `direct/human`, and it does not keep an approved commitment from execution.

## Consequences

- Monorepo: seven standalone eve apps (`agent-cmo` plus six specialist apps) join `apps/app` (console) and `apps/web`; there is no multi-agent `apps/agent` mount.
- `packages/brain`: `brand_id`-first signatures; typed preparation, approval, explicit pre-claim cancellation, dismissal, supersession, and result operations for commitment tasks.
- Console: the conversation-aware but stream-transparent `/eve/v1/*` proxy route, the browser-owned `useEveAgent` session store and cursor checkpoint mutation, the cache module, gated polling hooks, capability counters, and separate Plan controls for new-version Rebuild, same-Plan Ricontrolla/Advance, and exact-Move Avvia comunque.
- ADR-003 is amended; ADR-006's dual declaration remains, but its original same-deployment multi-agent mount is superseded by the seven-root topology above.
- Deployment configuration includes the CMO endpoint for the user proxy and all seven internal dispatch endpoints for cron fan-out. Credentials are capability-scoped and never substituted for one another:

  | Credential | Installed in | Sole authority |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | `CMO_BRIDGE_SECRET` | `apps/app`, `agent-cmo` | Mint and verify short-lived HS256 JWTs representing Better Auth human principals on the CMO proxy |
  | `DISPATCH_SECRET` | `apps/app`, all seven root deployments | Authenticate a raw Bearer request to `POST /internal/dispatch` |
  | `CRON_SECRET` | Vercel cron ingress, the `apps/app` cron route | Authenticate the scheduled invocation into `apps/app`; never forwarded |

  Specialist roots receive `DISPATCH_SECRET` but not `CMO_BRIDGE_SECRET`; compromising a specialist's dispatch credential therefore does not confer user-impersonation authority at the CMO boundary.
- Configuration contract tests pin `PROVIDER_HTTP_TIMEOUT = 90s` and `STALE_AFTER = 10m`. Deployment and Eve-upgrade verification observes that every root still uses the shared Fluid Function with the uncustomized 300-second Default Max Duration and no longer opt-in duration; it fails the rollout when that assumption changes and never repairs the generated Build Output artifact.
- CMO role-contract tests run the operation matrix against a current Member reloaded on every request. An exact owner with role `owner`, `admin`, or `member` may create its conversation and start/send turns, answer HITL, use session controls, checkpoint the cursor, and let an explicit answer be transduced once per exact Intent into a typed refinement; an exact owner downgraded to `viewer` may still read transcript/snapshot/stream but every first-send, follow-up, `inputResponses`, compact, clear, reset, cursor, and product mutation fails at its proxy/application boundary, with CMO-targeted requests never reaching the upstream agent. That viewer may cancel only with the exact non-empty `turnId` learned from its authorized stream; a missing, stale, or different id cannot affect a later turn, and successful cancel does not make the next send legal. A different same-organization Member—including an organization owner/admin—cannot list or load the conversation, read metadata or the retained Postgres fallback, obtain snapshot/stream/reconnect access, or reach Eve even when it knows the conversation/session/turn ids; removed Members, wrong organizations, mismatched sessions, child/task/autonomous contexts, and caller/model-supplied causal provenance likewise fail. The same fixture proves that such a non-owner Member can still read the ordinary same-brand canonical Action/Object projections produced from that private turn. The accepted refinement fixture asserts one CMO-authored `intent_refined` Action for `(conversation, session, turn, intent)`, an unchanged Intent author, trusted human/Member and conversation/session/turn/call provenance, exact same-turn replay even when the model emits another call id, no implicit task or Approval Action, and a further question rather than a write when the answer is ambiguous. The browser sends one answer at a time and never automatically retries a submitted `message` or `inputResponses`: Eve 0.31.3 treats a stale response as a new user message/new turn, which is intentionally outside the same-turn idempotency guarantee. Interactive specialist-request fixtures additionally prove that an exact “continue Intent X” or just-declared/refined X can create work without another click; multiple plausible Intents produce a question and zero task; task/autonomous/child/consultative/system wrappers accept no raw Intent selector; and the returned receipt exposes the exact preserved Intent revision without bypassing Policy, capability, credit or external-approval gates. A stale bridge JWT claiming a former write role loses to the current Member row. The catch-all fixture fails a future session subroute until it is explicitly classified as read, write, or scoped cancel, and asserts that no viewer model call or role-based prompt/tool filtering exists.
- Task-question route tests prove that every current organization Member may read the ordinary same-brand card, but only `owner | admin | member` can start its answer turn; the app reloads an exact `succeeded + partial|blocked + non-empty completion` source, uses only a conversation owned by that current user, stamps the source into trusted current-turn auth, and rejects wrong-brand, removed-Member, viewer-send, another owner's conversation, terminal-session reuse, model-supplied task id, and `clientContext` as authority. Opening/sending produces no resolution Action. The top-level CMO may close only the reloaded whole bundle through one receipt-first `task_questions_resolved`; same-turn replay with another Eve call id returns the first receipt because `call_id` is telemetry-only, a different semantic hash conflicts, and answering without an explicit “continue/retry” request creates no new work.
- CMO proxy contract tests prove that creation without the pre-created `conversation_id`, creation by a non-owner, creation without the exact `Member(brand.organization_id, user_id)`, every current session subroute for another owner or a brand in another organization, a conversation/session mismatch, and an unknown `sessionId` all fail without reaching the upstream agent. The proxy strips the conversation selector on create, follow-up, stream, and reconnect. Creation success requires a valid `x-eve-session-id`; the top-level hook and response path converge on the same fill-or-match binding before visible success, a child cannot bind, a different binding returns conflict without exposing the new id, and upstream status, non-hop-by-hop headers, body, and stream semantics are preserved. Transcript tests persist complete raw root and child `session_events` plus their billable charges, select only the ordered events whose exact emitting session matches `cmo_conversations.session_id`, and fold them through `defaultMessageReducer()`: the root `action.result(subagent-result)` yields exactly one dynamic-tool result, child `subagent.completed` and raw child events are absent, and any later root summary is a separate assistant part. Retention tests protect those exact root rows through the brand FK cascade without protecting descendants by `root_session_id`. Failure tests inject a hook-persistence miss, assert alerting and the resulting fallback gap, and prove snapshot/recovery does not write the missing event or charge; transcript reads cross the pagination boundary and prove a 2,000-row tail is not treated as complete. A version fixture replays representative stored streams and gates the shared `apps/app`/`agent-cmo` Eve upgrade. Stream tests prove the proxy does not consume NDJSON or own reconnect. Cursor tests accept only the exact bound session and apply `GREATEST`; recovery tests prove that the index-`0` snapshot plus any low-level drain completes before hook mount, stops explicitly at `isCurrentTurnBoundaryEvent`, and hydrates the hook with the combined events and final cursor even when the database checkpoint is stale. The table-driven session-route test includes follow-up send, stream, cancel, clear, compact, and reset, plus a catch-all assertion so a future `/session/:sessionId/*` subroute inherits the same guard.
- Schedule setup/configuration tests prove that concurrent `reconcileBrandSchedules` calls are insert-if-missing and never reset configuration or cursors; every row starts disabled with null timezone/cursor; only current `owner | admin | member` can configure it; exact replay returns the historical resulting snapshot before current template/revision/Policy guards, including after later reconfiguration or template retirement; different-hash reuse and stale revision write nothing; and model, task, consultation, system, viewer, wrong-tenant, unknown-template, or caller-authored worker/kind/payload/origin/cursor input is rejected. A retired template is visibly non-runnable: a receipt miss may only disable its currently-enabled row without changing calendar/cursor; re-enable, calendar edits, or a redundant disable write nothing. First configuration, every calendar edit, and re-enable use the same version-pinned calendar helper as dispatch to compute the first strictly future IANA wall-clock slot, so disabled time never catches up. Exact UTC fixtures cover normal daily/weekly slots, spring gaps by skipping that date, and autumn folds by selecting the earlier instant. Configuration and due materialization serialize on the revision: disable-first creates no occurrence, while materialize-first may leave one already accepted occurrence that disable never cancels.
- A deployment-contract fixture rejects retirement promotion of `apps/app` until the responsible root's production source contains the exact tombstone commit and 330 seconds have elapsed since its completed production-routing cutover under the verified 300-second Max Duration. A synthetic old invocation surviving to second 299 therefore keeps the console promotion blocked. Any reviewed Function-duration change recomputes this gate from the deployment verification rather than retaining 330 seconds. Root-first skew is allowed and merely stops new materialization before the old console wording catches up; console-first skew is a release failure. After both are live, every later root promotion is checked: a pre-retirement or otherwise executable manifest is rejected until a console-first rollback removes the tombstone wording, while a tombstone-compatible rollback passes. An occurrence accepted before quiescence may drain and remains visible without making the retired template executable. The permitted incompatible sequence is therefore root-first plus quiescence on promotion and console-first on rollback. No release step edits `schedules.enabled` or fabricates a Human Action.
- Separate route-contract tests verify that a production specialist returns `401` for `/eve/v1/info` and every `/eve/v1/session*` operation, keeps `/eve/v1/health` public, and cannot start work except after its authenticated dispatcher has claimed a task. Dispatch tests filter due rows by `SELF`, `compiledSupportedKinds`, and `compiledScheduleKeysForSelf` before the bounded scan; an unknown or mismatched template is untouched and cannot starve known keys. Concurrent pokes yield one cursor advance and at most one queued/running occurrence; a registry, Policy, Action, or insertion failure rolls the advance back; exact replay returns the original `(schedule_id, scheduled_for)` task; and the partial active-schedule index is the final defence. With no active occurrence, current null-structure Policy produces either one origin-free task plus `schedule_occurrence_evaluated(created)` or zero task plus `not_allowed`; every common-origin and lineage field is null. With an active or credit-blocked queued occurrence, the slot advances and atomically updates only the coalescing summary. The task-row race gives terminalization-before-lock permission to evaluate while materialization-before-terminalization coalesces; terminalization creates no immediate catch-up. Daily/weekly fixtures preserve local wall-clock time across both DST offset changes, task outcome never rewrites the schedule, and `scheduleRecheck` cannot edit it or borrow a product cadence.
- Lane tests verify that unsupported kinds remain untouched; an agent task always uses task mode and is never redispatched after authoritative `session_id`; a human external commitment requires its Approval Action and is never reclaimed after `running`; and a serialized commitment claim revalidates its trusted conflict key. Only the provider connector interprets HTTP/SDK outcomes and returns the closed `accepted | rejected | unknown` result; the generic dispatcher uses an exhaustive switch and maps unexpected throws to unknown; only the owner root classifies it after `STALE_AFTER` with an `unknown` Result Action in the same transaction as `outcome_unknown`. Given a constrained drain, no direct/human row becomes `running` until one worker slot is ready to enter its handler; `DRAIN_BATCH` counts started executions, the per-invocation concurrency bound is respected, lane preference is reevaluated per free slot, and concurrent invocations still claim each row at most once without pretending to enforce a global cap. Provider-outcome polls, approval conflicts, and retry-safe direct/automatic effects retain the dedicated ADR-018/ADR-019 fixtures. Local development verifies that `eve dev`, the TUI, and SDK diagnostics continue to work through `localDev()`.

## Alternatives considered

- **`withEve()` in-app** (original ADR-003) — superseded: the no-intelligence line loses physical enforcement exactly where drift is cheapest.
- **One standalone multi-agent eve deployment** (the first version of this ADR) — superseded: it conflates independently owned durable roots and relies on named-agent mount routing that the selected topology does not use.
- **One secret for bridge JWTs and dispatch pokes** (the CRM's `AGENT_BRIDGE_SECRET` shape) — rejected: all six specialist deployments would receive a credential capable of impersonating humans at the CMO boundary.
- **One dispatch secret per root or workload-identity/OIDC authentication** — not adopted in v1: a shared `DISPATCH_SECRET` exposes only a payload-free wake-up route whose called deployment still applies its compiled root and kind allowlists; seven rotations or an identity-provider integration add operational machinery without widening that route's authority.
- **Leave the specialist `eveChannel` implicit** — rejected: eve 0.31.3 enables the channel by default, whereas specialist production session routes must fail closed independently of framework defaults.
- **Disable the framework channel through a private route API** — rejected: the architecture targets only eve's supported public surface. `eveChannel({ auth: [localDev()] })` preserves local tooling and fails closed in production without relying on `disableRoute()` or another private seam.
- **Per-row `contract_version` plus a deployment coordinator** — not adopted in v1: the compiled kind allowlist makes version skew fail closed, while backward-compatible contracts and new kinds or expand-contract rollouts cover schema evolution without adding another queue state machine.
- **Let tasks and Actions survive brand deletion** — rejected by ADR-019: durable work survives process and deployment failure, not deletion of its tenant. Real foreign keys cascade every internal brand row; provider resources are deliberately outside that promise.
- **SSE from v1** — deferred to Phase 2; polling gated on in-flight work covers the console's needs.
