## Context

ADRs 001–010 left several cross-cutting operational rules either implicit or distributed across multiple documents. This ADR consolidates the seven invariants needed for those decisions to compose in production. It also records the rejected `skill_snapshot` idea and its reasoning.

## Decisions

### D1 — Consultation and durable work are different doors

[ADR-017](017-consultative-subagents-durable-root-work.md) owns the current contract. Declared specialist calls are read-only consultations and intentionally have no task dedup guarantee. Any work that must persist, write, retry, or execute enters the single durable task lane through the atomic task service; only a dispatcher-claimed named root executes it. There is no pre-delegation consultation lock and no second durable execution door.

### D2 — The CMO's job list; the daily brief is a task kind

After ADR-010 the CMO's perimeter is written down explicitly:

1. **Transduction** — turning the current human's explicit request into a human-authored `active` Intent and turning that human's explicit, unambiguous CMO reply into typed criteria/constraints on an exact Intent revision. The reply itself authorizes the refinement; only the resulting Action is canonical. Outside the authenticated top-level interactive boundary an autonomous agent may only create an agent-authored `draft` proposal and cannot invoke the human-reply refinement branch
2. **Routing** — one self-contained brief to exactly one specialist
3. **Synthesis** — specialist outputs back to the human
4. **Recommending** typed roadmap inputs for explicit human Decision recording
5. **Generating, rebuilding, and routing** the structured Marketing Plan from an exact Strategy head, including recording the Evidence and Move Candidates needed by that synthesis and creating its internal/preparatory specialist work
6. **The daily brief narrative**

Plan generation is a normal CMO `agent/automatic` task, not console intelligence or a free direct handler. It uses Eve and the ordinary credit gate. The CMO may commit task-attributed Evidence and Move Candidates, then calls the atomic publication boundary owned by ADR-012. That tool's producing-Action receipt records exact inputs, route mappings, and exclusions; `TaskCompletion` references only the resulting Plan. ADR-021 owns later waves and readiness recovery, while ADR-019 keeps every external commitment behind its own human button.

Move dependencies are validated, acyclic Plan semantics; they do not gate task claims. The submitted route list is the wave the CMO judges startable from currently available Objects and facts, not every Move in the roadmap. A dependent Move left without a route remains visible. ADR-021's CMO advancement task may later select it against the same Plan, and an authorized human may request another evaluation or override the CMO's readiness judgment for that exact Move. Automatic signals are best-effort rather than a hidden dependency engine: a terminal descendant may ring the same evaluation bell only when its immutable same-brand/null-Intent/exact Plan/Move parent chain reaches a route adopted by the current Plan. This ancestry neither proves Move completion nor grants routing or external authority. Task failure causes no dependency cascade, and external commitments remain governed only by ADR-019's human boundary.

The CMO model owns no scheduling decision for the daily brief. A deterministic per-brand `schedules` row advances each due evaluation for the CMO root and materializes a daily-brief task only when that schedule has no queued/running occurrence; otherwise the slot coalesces without backlog. A materialized daily brief is then dispatched like any other task. The brief is a **mechanical skeleton** (yesterday's actions, pending items, due rechecks) plus CMO narrative whose **citations are validated mechanically** — every claim must reference existing object ids, and the renderer rejects invalid references. This is the rule that keeps "digest with citations" (our stated differentiator) safe from hallucination.

### D3 — Decision-impact verification closes into work, not just data

This section concerns analytics and marketing impact, not ADR-019's provider-outcome polling. A provider Verification records whether an accepted command later completed, failed, or became unverified; by itself it neither judges nor contests the Decision that motivated the command. ADR-020 closes the feedback loop with an executable contract:

- Every canonical `roadmap_input` declares either `impact.mode = not_applicable` or one complete provider-independent `MetricImpactPlan`: semantic metric definition/scope, decimal baseline and same-brand agent-authored Evidence plus window, normalized absolute target and comparator, observation window, and absolute future `evaluate_at`. An incomplete measurable recommendation remains task/conversation output and an open question; it is not committed as a malformed or "low-structure" Decision.
- The human `recordDecision` transaction that records a measurable head also creates exactly one future origin-free `verify-roadmap-decision-impact` task for Growth, due no earlier than `evaluate_at`. The `agent/automatic` task passes through the ordinary credit gate, may use any available measurement read tools, records its interpreted observation with `record_evidence`, and stages the common `TaskCompletion.status = 'completed'`, `intent_acceptance = null`, with a kind-specific `supported`, `not_supported`, or `inconclusive` judgment. Settlement atomically records that judgment in the Growth-authored Verification Action and never changes the originating Intent. Technical delivery/model failure follows the ordinary one-shot agent lifecycle and never becomes a marketing judgment.
- `contested` is a console projection over a still-active Decision with a `not_supported` Verification, not an Object status or content mutation. That same negative settlement creates or observes one registered CMO reconsideration task, whose closed result is either `recommended(report_object_id)` or `obsolete(decision_inactive)`. The CMO recommends a motivated replacement; only a later explicit human `recordDecision(expectedHeadId)` may supersede the head. Its immutable origin Intent remains causal provenance even if it later becomes terminal: the brand-wide Strategy and recommendation stay actionable while the exact Decision head is current. A negative Strategy judgment never mutates, reopens, or borrows authority from that Intent. It is not an Intent-acceptance result.

### D4 — Budget hierarchy: plan allowance > brand pool > per-task work budget

Three budget layers inherited from three sources now have an explicit nesting:

- **Plan allowance** (billing admission threshold) — the global autonomous-spend policy of ADR-006
- **Brand monthly pool** (from the subscription; the Magister credit model)
- **Per-task work budget** (selected from `budgetClass` by policy at dispatch; autonomous runs get less). This is an application soft budget exposed in the brief and enforceable at authored tool boundaries, not a dynamic eve token limit

The dispatcher checks the brand pool **only before claiming `execution_mode = agent` tasks**: at zero or below, those tasks stay queued and the console shows "N jobs waiting on credits" — credits are a capability like any other, rendered with the ADR-009 counter pattern. The check is a best-effort admission gate, not a reservation: seven independently scaled roots may all observe the same positive balance and claim different rows. Each winning billable `step.completed` posts its own `model_charge`, so already-running sessions may take the ledger negative before later claims stop. V1 accepts that bounded concurrent overage instead of adding credit holds, maximum-cost reservations, or a global brand semaphore. The gate is not part of a generic task-claim helper. Deterministic direct lanes do not start an AI session; in particular, a `direct/human` commitment already authorized by its Approval Action is claimed even when the pool is zero. Only billable `succeeded` with a stable receipt produces the idempotent `action_charge`/paid overage; ambiguous or unsuccessful outcomes do not. If the specialist sees its soft work budget ending in time, it may deliberately save partial work, stage `TaskCompletion(partial)`, and exit successfully. That is a proactive domain result, not a guarantee of the runtime cap.

Compaction does not participate in any of these commercial layers. Eve 0.31.3 may use zero, one, or multiple provider calls for an automatic or manual compaction, but its `compaction.requested`/`compaction.completed` events carry no authoritative billable usage, cost, or Gateway generation id and never emit the `step.completed` required by the ledger. Those calls are Branderize platform COGS: no `model_charge`, credit consumption, reservation, or admission adjustment is inferred. The active model's Gateway attribution remains enabled so Custom Reporting can measure the overhead; the local ledger remains authoritative for billable application charges and admission, not total provider spend. V1 adds no estimate, generation lookup, framework fork, OpenTelemetry billing path, or reconciler.

Each standalone root declares one static eve input/output token limit in `defineAgent`; eve 0.31.3 does not accept per-task limits through the channel `send()` options. The call that crosses the cap is allowed to finish, and a task-mode run fails when it needs another model call. `SESSION_TOKEN_LIMIT_REACHED` is therefore a technical task failure, exactly like the current CRM AgentRun boundary, and is never translated into synthetic `partial`. The static cap is a high safety fuse with headroom above ordinary task work budgets, not one of the commercial credit layers.

### D5 — Proposal shape: a versioned task and derived preview

> **Amended by ADR-019.** An executable proposal is no longer an Object. It is the same direct task that will later execute, initially in `awaiting_approval`.

- Every commitment task kind supplies a typed payload and trusted preview renderer. `kind`, worker, effect class, connector operation, and renderer are registry-derived rather than payload-selected.
- **Edit-before-approve is supported.** An edit valid for the same kind increments the task revision and records the human delta as an Action. Approval re-derives Policy from that final revision; a stale click fails.
- V1 deliberately has no generic provider-state readback/hash gate and no pinned provider account. The human click authorizes the current registered operation through the brand's current active connection. Provider-side edits or account changes made by the user before clicking are their responsibility.

### D6 — Exactly one scheduler poke; binaries in content-addressed blobs

- **One scheduler poke, no Eve schedules.** A single Vercel Cron in `apps/app` fans out in parallel to the CMO and six standalone specialist roots; it decides nothing and waits only for their `202` acknowledgments. Every root owns an authenticated custom-channel `POST /internal/dispatch` route. Before draining tasks, the root evaluates its own due deterministic `schedules` under compare-and-swap and always advances a successfully evaluated cursor. With no `queued | running` occurrence and an origin still eligible for new work it creates the exact one-shot `(schedule_id, scheduled_for)` task; with one it creates no task and updates only the schedule's monotonic coalescing counter and last suppressed slot; with no occurrence but an inactive Intent it advances with zero task. Exact occurrence uniqueness protects replay, a partial active-schedule unique index enforces at most one nonterminal occurrence, and a task-row lock gives terminalization-before-materialization permission to insert while materialization-before-terminalization coalesces. The daily brief at 09:00 and weekly SEO audit are wall-clock calendar rules in the brand's configured IANA timezone, never fixed-minute intervals and never self-rescheduling task rows. After downtime with no active occurrence and an eligible origin, only the oldest missed slot becomes a task and the cursor advances directly beyond `now`; with an active or credit-blocked occurrence, later due slots leave no backlog and terminalization creates no immediate catch-up. The committed schedule advance is independent of that task's eventual outcome. After claiming an agent row, the drain starts task mode through Eve 0.31.3's public `from(taskAddress).send(...)` operation; the address is derived from the trusted task id, while the returned immutable `session_id` becomes the authoritative run binding. Each root touches only schedule/task rows whose registry-derived `worker_key` equals itself. A first cross-kind `scheduleRecheck` booking commits at the tool call; only an equivalent running self-recheck stages `next_*` and is success-coupled. Both are separate from deterministic schedules. Only retry-safe direct/automatic kinds retain leases and `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`; they are limited to transaction-safe internal work or side-effect-free idempotent provider reads. A human external commitment never reclaims after it starts (ADR-009, ADR-014, ADR-018, ADR-019).
- **Binary artifacts** (images, video, PDFs) live in Vercel Blob under a brand-scoped content-addressed key containing the full byte hash. One `artifact` Object represents one binary and owns one `blob_key`; a document such as Brand Context references artifact ids plus their typed roles rather than embedding remote URLs or multiple blob keys.
- A remote asset is mirrored in this order: safe server-side download with MIME, size, redirect, and timeout limits → hash the validated bytes → idempotent upload at the deterministic key → only then commit the Artifact Object and every canonical reference to it. Download and upload happen outside the Postgres transaction. Upload replay of the same bytes/key is success. If the later database transaction fails, an unreferenced Blob may remain; v1 accepts that orphan and may sweep it later. The reverse state — a canonical Object referring to bytes not yet present — is forbidden.
- Context.dev source URLs remain evidence/provenance only. If one optional logo variant cannot be mirrored, that Artifact is omitted while the rest of Brand Context v0 may still commit; the UI never silently promotes the remote URL into the canonical asset. The console serves canonical assets through the authenticated Blob-delivery path rather than trusting the upstream URL.

### D7 — Human Policy roles come from the current organization Member

Multi-user organizations need approval rights per role. A Better Auth User has exactly one global human Actor, reused across every organization; the Actor has no tenant fields and supplies only identity and audit attribution. The boundary resolves the requested brand's `organization_id`, reads the exact Better Auth `Member(organization_id, user_id)`, materializes or reloads the human Actor by that same `user_id`, and supplies the Member's current organization-wide role to Policy. `actors.role` is not an authorization source. The resulting brand-scoped Action's `policy_snapshot` freezes the organization, role, and verdict used. The v1 organization-wide matrix is fixed code: **owner/admin approve everything, members approve the non-financial, viewers are read-only**. A brand-scoped `policy_restriction` Decision may only tighten that authority for its brand; it cannot override the organization role matrix or expand authority. A viewer is still an organization Member with read-only product access across its brands, not a brand-membership class. ADR-016's exact-owner, exact-`turnId` cancellation of an already observed CMO turn is the sole state-reducing exception and creates no Action, follow-up, or product authority. Four-eyes on `financial` (approver != intent author) is deferred to Phase 3. Cross-member conversation-transcript visibility remains separate under ADR-016.

## Rejected: skill versions in action provenance

The grilling floated recording a `skill_snapshot` on Actions, since a marketingskills submodule update changes agent behavior. **Rejected by the maintainer:** skills are content chosen at deploy time, exactly like application code — and the app deploy is not in `policy_snapshot` either. `policy_snapshot` exists because policy is *evaluated at runtime* on every action and must be replayable; skills are static inputs versioned by git. Syncing with upstream is a deliberate choice, not an obligation: the submodule starts from Haines' collection because it is good and verified, and may later be replaced by self-written skills.
